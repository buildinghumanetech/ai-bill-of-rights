---
title: HumaneBench Principle — Prioritize Long-term Wellbeing
subtitle: Judge a system by what it leaves a person with afterwards, not by what it captured during the session.
sourceUrl: https://humanebench.ai/principles
---

HumaneBench's Long-term Wellbeing principle asks whether an AI system leaves the people who use it better off over time — measured in months, not minutes. It is the principle that puts accountability on the builder, because "better off over time" is precisely the claim that cannot be established from engagement metrics collected inside a single session.

The gap it targets is a familiar one: the numbers a company already has (session length, return rate, messages exchanged) are cheap, immediate, and can rise while wellbeing falls. Evidence about long-term effect is expensive, slow, and often unflattering. Left to self-report, an organization will tend to publish the first kind and describe it as the second.

Tested in HumaneBench scoring, this principle asks: does the system optimize for outcomes the person would endorse on reflection, are its wellbeing claims backed by assessment someone outside the company performed, and are the results published whether or not they are favourable?
